A Slack client for Mac that organises conversations into topics and decides which messages are worth a notification.

Download for Mac For Apple Silicon Macs.

One list of topics

A topic is a Slack thread, or the unthreaded messages of a channel or DM. Juno lists them newest first, and Resolve removes one from the list until someone replies.

Nothing waits for Slack

Juno reads every screen from a copy of your Slack on this Mac, so opening a topic, switching a tab or ⌘K never waits for the network. Rows and tabs open on mouse down, and hovering a row loads its topic before the click. A message you send shows at once and goes to Slack in the background, in order. Every interaction has a time budget, and a change that breaks one fails the build.

Pleasant to look at

Juno follows macOS: native menus, the arrow cursor on every button and row, and a pressed button that stays still. Unread counts are quiet tags in your accent colour, not black badges, and with an AI key Juno draws a small picture for each row. When you are caught up, Home shows a painting or print from a museum's collection, or a picture from space, with one line about it. Light and dark themes, and a choice of accent colours.

Notifications decided for you

Each message either notifies you or stays in Juno. Juno reads it with your instructions in your own words, and keeps every decision with its reason.

Home

Home lists questions you have not answered and things you said you would do, with links to their topics. It also suggests topics to resolve.

Ask Juno

Ask Juno to catch you up on a topic, and the answer links to the messages it read. Ask it to draft a reply, and it waits for your click before sending.

Also ⌘K for conversations and messages already on this Mac, a Search tab that searches Slack with filters, tabs like a browser, your own views of channels and DMs, Slack huddles, a blur for message text while you share your screen, and web links in a side panel.

Getting started

  1. Download the -arm64.dmg from the latest release and drag Juno to Applications.
  2. If macOS blocks the first open, choose Open Anyway in System Settings › Privacy & Security, or run: 
    xattr -d com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Juno.app
  3. Sign in to Slack's desktop app first. In Juno, choose "Use the Slack app's session" and allow the macOS password prompt.
  4. For the AI features, add an OpenRouter key in Settings › Juno. These features send the relevant Slack text to OpenRouter, or to the endpoint you chose for the assistant. Without a key, direct messages and mentions notify.
