Judge the conversation, not the message.

Almost everything, from DMs to topics, is set to Auto, so Auto is what has to be right. Today Auto asks whether one message sounds urgent. That holds chit-chat in a conversation you're in, and can't tell a bare "hey" from a real ask. This version looks at the conversation first, then reads the message with your own instructions, and has two outcomes: notify, or stay in Juno.

Two outcomesA banner, which stays in Notification Center if you're away, or it waits in Juno. Nothing in between.
Your words in the readingWhen Juno reads a message and it clearly falls under your instructions, Juno does what they say.
Conversation, not messageA reply in a conversation you're in notifies, even if it's chit-chat. A bare "hey" doesn't.
Real words, clear reasonsBanners show what people wrote. Every call has a one-line reason you can correct.

How Auto decides

For everything on Auto, Juno checks in this order:
  1. Closers. "thanks", "ok" and the like don't notify.
  2. Conversations you're in. If you wrote in the last 10 minutes, replies notify.
  3. Juno reads it. If it clearly falls under your instructions, Juno does what they say. Otherwise asks, answers to your questions, follow-ups you haven't answered and serious problems notify. Greetings, FYIs and chatter wait in Juno.

The AI's job

One classifier call, not a chat model

Juno already calls Jev (typesafe/jev-1.13) for decisions: about 0.5 s, about $0.03 per 1,000 calls, and 100% on the bench. Jev takes structured state and typed questions, and every answer comes with probabilities. So it gives its own confidence, and it can ask about your instructions and whether the message needs you in the same call. No chat model is needed on this path.

What it reads (the state)

  • The new message, and any that arrived with it
  • The thread's first message and up to 10 earlier messages, yours marked, with times
  • Facts: whether you wrote last and how long ago, whether you started the thread, DM or channel
  • How often you talk to the sender, counted, never a guessed org chart
  • Your instructions, each with its direction

Your instructions are already a list

Each instruction is saved with its direction, Notify me or Don't notify, so nothing needs splitting or guessing. Each message needs one question: which instruction, if any, covers it? Why quotes that instruction. When you add one, a single chat-model call checks whether it is really a rule about one person or channel ("Found a rule"); that is once per instruction, never per message.

When part of a message matches

"hey! can you approve the contract by 3?" starts with a greeting, but it is mainly an ask. Jev is asked what each message is mainly about, so "Don't notify: greetings" doesn't match it. A Don't notify instruction also needs a higher confidence than a Notify me one, because holding something that needed you costs more than one extra banner. A borderline case goes on to whether it needs you.

Existing free text

The list is a new setting. The old What matters to you text is left where it is and no longer read, so nothing breaks for anyone who set it. It isn't converted.

Why lines without generated text

Jev picks from fixed options and doesn't write sentences. Why is built from the option and the facts, for example "Sam asks you something." or "Dev is following up, and you haven't replied." It is less specific than generated text, but it never invents anything.

Uncertain or slow

If no instruction clears the bar, the instructions don't decide. "Needs you" adds up the probabilities of the options that notify and compares the total with one threshold. Both bars come from your replay labels, not from guesses. A chat model is used only if the replay shows Jev's uncertain cases are frequent and a chat model gets them right. If the call takes more than 2 s, DMs and mentions notify and the rest stays in Juno.

// One Jev call
state: {
  message, earlier, facts,
  instructions: { i1: "Notify me: anything on the Q4 launch",
                  i2: "Don't notify: social plans" }
}
questions: {
  instruction: { type: "choice",            // only if you wrote any
    criteria: {
      i1: "The main point of this message is: anything on the Q4 launch",
      i2: "The main point of this message is: social plans",
      none: "The main point is something else, even if part of it touches an instruction" } },
  needsYou: { type: "choice",
    criteria: {
      ask:       "Asks the owner to reply, act or decide",
      answer:    "Answers something the owner asked",
      follow_up: "Follows up on an ask the owner hasn't answered",
      decision:  "A decision the owner should weigh in on",
      serious:   "Something serious the owner is part of",
      greeting:  "A greeting with nothing asked yet",
      fyi:       "An update with nothing asked of the owner",
      chatter:   "Talk the owner isn't part of" } }
}

// Answers come with probabilities
instruction → { choice: "i2", confidence: 0.94, probabilities }
needsYou    → { choice: "ask", confidence: 0.81, probabilities }

// How Juno applies it
only a closer                     → stay in Juno
you wrote ≤ 10 min ago            → notify
Notify me match, conf ≥ bar       → notify
Don't notify match, conf ≥ higher bar → stay in Juno
P(ask+answer+follow_up+decision+serious) ≥ bar → notify
otherwise                         → stay in Juno

Measuring

Test it on your own messages first

Before it ships

Replay your last few weeks of Slack, in time order, through today's check and the new one. You label about 300 sampled messages: should it have notified, yes or no. Compare both against your labels. Then ship it, log both side by side for two weeks, and treat your corrections in Why as more labels.

What good looks like

9 in 10 notifications are ones you'd keep. 95% of your "yes" labels notify. Fewer notifications than today at the same recall, not just fewer. Instructions followed every time they clearly apply. Zero notifications outside hours, zero duplicates.

Open questions

Small decisions left

The Replies switch is gone. Fine?

"Replies where you wrote recently" skipped Juno's reading, so "ok" notified too. Check 3 in Auto already covers live replies, and a thread that matters can be set to Always.

Recommendation: remove it.

How long is "a conversation you're in"?

10 minutes since you last wrote. Long enough for a slow back-and-forth, short enough that an old chat goes back to the normal checks.

Recommendation: start at 10 and check it against your history: how often a reply came within 10 minutes of your last message.

How long does a burst count as one banner?

Banners are never updated once shown. After one, more messages in the same conversation don't notify again for a short while.

Recommendation: 1 minute. Anything in that minute is in Juno when you open the conversation.

What counts as a clear match with your instructions?

A loose match, such as "social plans" against a team offsite, could hold something you wanted. Only a clear match decides; a loose one falls back to whether it needs you.

Recommendation: set the bar from the replay labels, and quote the instruction in Why so a wrong match is easy to spot and fix.

Later ideas, not in this version

  • Pause notifications for an hour, set in Slack, so others can see you're paused too.
  • Holding things that can wait until a natural break, such as unlocking or leaving a huddle.
  • Watching busy channels you're not part of, such as an incident channel heating up.
