# Juno sync protocols

These specs describe the protocols and their limits. The model checks test the specs; `bun run specs:conformance` checks that the engine follows `outbox.qnt` ("Conformance" below).

`follow.qnt` models the current follow command seam, two threads inside the quiet window, and one older thread. It includes Slack writes, socket delivery, HTTP pages, boot and hourly window passes, shorter reconnect reads, and activity lookups. Its `followProtocol` module takes constants; `follow` supplies the full simulator instance. `readstate.qnt` models read state as implemented in M4: Main and one followed thread that mirror, and one unfollowed thread that stays local. Its `readProtocol` module takes constants; `readstate` supplies the full simulator instance and `readstate-bounded.qnt` a small one. Like follow, it keeps Slack's event clock and Juno's observation clock apart. `outbox.qnt` models message sends, ambiguous failures, restart recovery and reconciliation, and edits, deletes and reaction toggles on Juno's own posts. Its `outboxProtocol` module takes constants; `outbox` supplies two draft IDs. `catchup.qnt` models messages that other clients post, edit, delete and react to while the socket is down, and the recheck after the reconnect; `catchupProtocol` takes constants and `catchup` supplies three messages. `threadranges.qnt` models which replies of one thread Juno has proven it holds (architecture 5.5 and 5.6, `@juno/sync` `threadRanges.ts`). Replies are numbered by their ts and the root is 0. Pages of `conversations.replies` hold two replies after an exclusive `oldest` and up to an optional `latest` (always a stored message, so never later than now). Each page lands with the range it proved, up to its last reply or its `latest` bound, never to a clock. Reply events reach Juno in the order Slack sent them and only while the socket is up. A drop (a restart is the same here) loses the ones in flight and ends every live tail: the range closes at the newest reply the socket delivered on that connection (`heard`), a followed thread's tail becomes `held` and an unfollowed thread's `none`; unfollowing ends a held tail. Ranges merge only where pages of one kind touch, so a live tail never joins the ranges above it. `view` is a `getView` row for a followed thread: every reply after the read pointer, in a `view` range that counts for the unread count only. `message_replied` is left out: it can arrive late or not at all, so the root's count is the maximum of what pages said and the replies stored.

Properties: `rangesProven` (every reply a range covers is stored), `liveProven` (every reply after a live range's start is stored or on its way), `shownContinuous` (two stored replies one page range covers have every reply between them stored, or for that moment on the socket) and `countSound` (an exact unread count misses no reply Juno knows exists). `countSound` leaves out one assumption: after a drop, while a followed thread's count rests on a held tail alone and the reconnect's view is still due (the ghost `viewDue`), it trusts that view to bring the unread replies, as Juno did before ranges. The model does not cover clock skew (no range depends on a clock), replies Juno sends itself, the live-root and cold-boot seed ranges, the deletion recount, or the engine's window clipping; the engine flow tests in `thread-coverage.flow.test.ts` do.

Directed pairs, each corrected schedule ending in agreement and each bug mode the rule Juno had before: `liveCount` (`thread-live-count`) loses a reply in a drop, then a live reply lifts the stored count to the root's, and the old count rule, trusting `reply_count`, violates `countSound`. `jumpGap` (`thread-jump-gap`) stores the start of a thread, jumps deep into it and steps back up; with no ranges the replies on either side of the hole look continuous and violate `shownContinuous`. `restartGap` (`thread-restart-gap`) closes the app, posts a reply and opens it again; old builds kept the thread's coverage across a restart, which violates `countSound`. `followedDropStep` is corrected only: the held tail, then the reconnect's view. The runner also samples 10,000 random traces of at most 60 transitions: at most six replies are posted, and longer traces only repeat deliveries and pages past the job's 60-second budget.

## Accepted Unsubscribe: three races and their guards

Each race is a directed schedule with a fixed mode (`<name>Step`, must satisfy `invariants`) and a bug mode (`<name>BugStep`, must violate `acceptedUnfollow`). The bug modes are the rules Juno had before the guards.

`lateEvent` (`accepted-unsubscribe-late-event`): `officialFollow(1, 1)` creates a followed event at Slack stamp 1 and its delivery is delayed; `unsubscribe(1)`, `accept(1)` and `acknowledge(1)` follow, with no echo. The delayed event passes the event watermark (`1 >= updatedEvent = 0`). In bug mode it applies and local becomes 1 while Slack says 0. The guard: the row is `commanded` and the event contradicts it, so it queues a root lookup, which reads 0. This can happen on an ordered socket too, since a command need not have an echo.

`pendingPage` (`accepted-unsubscribe-pending-page`): `unsubscribe(1)` writes local 0; a page starts while Slack still says 1; Slack accepts and Juno acknowledges; the page completes with 1. Its request started after the command's first stamp. In bug mode it applies. The guard: `acknowledge` stamps `observedAt` again, so the page is older than the row.

`failedLookup` (`accepted-unsubscribe-failed-lookup`): `unsubscribe(1)`, `accept(1)`, then `reject(1)` for the lost response, then `failLookup(1)`. In bug mode the compare-and-swap fallback restores the previous follow although Slack says 0. The guard: the row keeps the command's state and the lookup is retried; under `seal` it succeeds and reads 0.

The stale-page regression (`stale-follow-page`) is the older race: a page requested before the command. The command's first stamp stops it; its bug mode has no page guard at all.

## Run the checks and bounds

From the worktree root, run `bun run remote specs:check`. Quint checks run on the laptop; do not run them on the Mac. `specs:check` remains the verification entry point and queues behind `scripts/heavy.mjs`. Single-sample schedules from each spec share one Quint process and one parse/typecheck. The adapter uses the pinned CLI’s `runSimulator` with the original arguments for each schedule, including its init, main module, invariant, seed, witnesses, sample count and step bound. Each invocation creates a fresh evaluator and RNG; Rust still runs in a separate evaluator subprocess for each schedule. No schedule is selected by chance or omitted. Multi-sample runs and standalone typechecks keep their CLI processes. `SPECS_JOBS` sets how many simulation groups and typechecks run at once. The largest groups start first. Each schedule retains its own cache entry, output checks and ITF filename; a changed spec or imported spec invalidates its checks. `--no-cache` or `--env SPECS_CACHE=off` runs everything (see "Faster test runs" in `docs/development.md`).

Quint is pinned to `@informalsystems/quint@0.33.0`. The script typechecks every `.qnt`, samples all three full sync protocols 10,000 times with at most 160 transitions and two simulator threads, and uses seed `20261013` by default. Set `SPECS_SEED=<integer>` to reproduce another run. Full runs reuse unchanged inputs and runtime/platform identity; nightly runs choose and record a varied seed, while deterministic checks keep their cache. Outbox conformance runs only when its inputs change. Completed simulations stop at agreement. Directed schedules run once for at most 80 transitions, with guarded and bug modes for stale follow pages, the three accepted-Unsubscribe races, missing stored follows, a follow beyond the first window page, reordered follow events, rollback after a newer event, stale read observations, lost pending read observations, and peek. Additional schedules cover Unknown, observations retained through pending commands, ambiguous command failure, fallback, and old threads with and without activity. Read has guarded and bug modes for a late official mark (`lateMark`), a page from before a mark (`latePage`), a page requested while a mark was pending (`pendingPage`), an official mark during a pending one (`pendingMark`) and peek, plus `coalesceStep`, `retryAfterAcceptStep` and `peekThenMarkStep`. Outbox adds the nine bug pairs and six additional corrected schedules listed above. Generated ITF JSON and Apalache output in `specs/traces/` are ignored by git.

With Java, the bounded Apalache 0.62.1 checks use these small instances:

The four-transition check is a shallow safety check, not a convergence or exhaustive protocol proof. Long recovery and read bug traces are checked by simulation, not thirty-step symbolic exploration. The whole script has a twelve-minute execution budget, excluding the machine-lock wait. ## Read and map a trace

ITF `states[0]` is initial. In each state find the variable ending in `::s`, then read `label`, `target`, `local`, `server`, `observedAt` and `updatedEvent`. `#bigint`, `#map` and `#set` encode integers, maps and sets. `--mbt` records selected actions and arguments. For outbox, read `row`, `stored`, `server`, `request`, `due` and `fetches`, for actions `act`, `actKind`, `stale`, `actSent` and `acted`, and for network copies `dup`, `deletes` and `copyRow`. `server` counts Juno's accepted submissions per ID. All identifiers and stamps are invented.

| Follow action | Engine or fake Slack event |
| --- | --- |
| `subscribe`, `unsubscribe` / `localChange` | Local transaction, pending `follow` row, then add/remove call |
| `accept`, `acknowledge` | Fake Slack accepts the call (`holdNext` with `before` delays this); later release its success response, which stamps `observed_at` again |
| `reject` | Return any command error, possibly after acceptance (`loseResponse`); remove pending, stamp `observed_at` and ask for the root |
| `startFetch`, `finishFetch` kind `failure` or `success` | Failure refresh, or refresh after a noted observation on success |
| `failLookup` | Fail the root lookup too; the row keeps the command's state and the lookup is retried in the background |
| `officialFollow`, `threadMarked`, `reply` | Official add/remove, explicit `subscription.active`, or own-reply follow observation |
| `deliver`, `undatedEvent` | Deliver an event with its independent Slack stamp (`holdFrames` delays it), or queue a lookup for a missing stamp; a contradicting event on a commanded row also queues a lookup |
| `missingRoot` | Root without `subscribed`; it makes no flag write |
| `startFetch`, `finishFetch` kind `view` or `root` | Hold/release a getView page or explicit root observation |
| `startScan`, `requestPage`, `advancePage`, `finishScan` | Boot/hourly window pass or shorter reconnect list; only the window pass probes absences |
| `otherReply` | Another user's reply activates an old thread and checks a stale stored follow |
| `age` | Passage of the quiet window makes an old observation stale |
| `hourlyPass` | Make the next window pass due |
| `disconnect`, `reconnect`, `seal` | Drop/restore the socket; stop input and allow quiet recovery |

| Read action | Engine or fake Slack event |
| --- | --- |
| `markRead`, `markUnread` / `localChange` | `reads.markRead`, `reads.markUnread`: local transaction and the topic's `mark` row |
| `accept` | `MarkWorker` sends `conversations.mark` or `subscriptions.thread.mark`; the fake's handler runs (`holdNext` holds the answer) |
| `respond` | Release the answer; `endMark` |
| `retry`, `reject` | `loseResponse` or a transient error (`forceError internal_error`, `force429`); a refusal (`forceError` with another error) |
| `officialMark`, `deliver` | `markRead` / `markThreadRead` from another client, and its frame (`holdFrames` delays it) |
| `startFetch` kind `page`, `scan`, `lookup`, `finishFetch` | `client.counts` or a `getView` row; the reconnect's counts; `conversations.info` or a root lookup |
| `peek` | A peek at a topic (`containers/Peek.tsx`): it reads the topic's queries and fetches what the store lacks (`topics.open`), and runs no read command |

Network acceptance, response, event delivery and fetch completion are separate transitions in both models. Engine tests disable the periodic sweep timer and trigger reconnects explicitly. `syncIdle()` waits for scan completion, including a due follow pass, and `commandIdle()` waits for command recovery to finish applying its answer. These observation hooks do not change the protocol actions or their ordering rules.

## Maintain the protocol

Read the sync README's follow and read rule tables and architecture section 5 before changing a rule. Keep the event and observation clocks separate. Add a directed schedule for a new race, show the broken rule fails, run `bun run specs:check`, and document bounds and any expected implementation violations. Product rules remain in the public specs.

## Juno Agent memory (WP2)

`agent-memory.qnt` describes [plan v3](../docs/juno-agent/plan-v3.md), sections 3–5 and 15. It is a protocol contract for the future implementation, not a claim about existing application code. `memoryProtocol` supplies every transition; `agent_memory` supplies the simulator and directed schedules, and `agent_memory_bounded` supplies the small Apalache instance in the same file.

### Records, transactions and abstractions

- A durable inbox count precedes ordered projection into a dense archive. A source ID denotes an immutable opaque byte string. Redaction replaces its payload with a tombstone without reusing the ID. Each ID represents one chunk, up to 16,000 bytes; attachment storage and byte-page iteration are abstracted by that exact payload. Archive incarnations and two-journal power loss belong to WP4.
- Tree keys are aligned `(start, width)` intervals. Immutable node versions record source IDs, exact child versions and raw context source IDs. `deps` caches the transitive source closure of those recorded reads. The separate `read` field records what the job actually saw, so a missing dependency is detectable. Model output may omit every fact, copy a read source, invent a fact, exceed 512 bytes, or fail. Provenance is conservative even when the output omits a source. Nothing here proves recall, factual accuracy, or instruction authority.
- `start`, `complete`, and `publish` are separate transitions. New messages, pin edits, removal and crashes can intervene. Publication revalidates inputs, the live cutoff, redaction generation, then reads the current head revision in the same transaction as node insertion and head advancement. Stale results leave durable work queued. A crash loses jobs and request preparation but preserves the archive, queue, deadlines, pins and exact committed head.
- The persisted head names exact node versions, ordered lines, a coverage watermark and a generation. Masked lines retain their intervals and render a fixed placeholder. The tail is everything after the published prefix. Out-of-order completed leaves wait until the prefix reaches them. Only sibling lines with a clean built parent can merge. Priorities use integer cross multiplication, age from the pair's inclusive last message, and oldest-start tie breaking.
- A compaction reads raw archive sources before its interval, newest first, stopping before the first source whose size plus framing would exceed 16,384 bytes. Removed sources are skipped. Context IDs are recorded and have no transitive inputs. Compaction reads no pins. Direct compaction input is at most 32,768 bytes; all compaction calls also pass an admission bound. The model keeps the shortest of at most five output attempts. A stored node is at most 16,384 bytes: oversized leaves copy their source, and oversized parents cut their shortest attempt. A parent therefore reads at most 32,768 bytes. Compaction admission uses a 131,072-token model window. Each shortening call reuses the bounded original input and fixed template, revalidates freshness, and passes the same provider bound; it does not accumulate previous attempts into a transcript. Deadlines use two abstract clock ticks instead of the production backoff ladder; the clock and worker advance without new messages.
- Pins are trusted copies of an owner source span (at most 280 bytes) or explicit owner edits. Edits preserve source lineage and supersede the previous version. Admission enforces 4,096 active bytes atomically. A removal deactivates matching pins immediately, including owner-edited descendants; delayed edits recheck lineage. The span's integer payload abstracts exact byte slicing, not its implementation or UTF-8 validation.
- Removal increments the generation, masks precisely the affected published lines, queues affected node keys, removes pins, invalidates prepared requests and clears affected live report/retrieval handles in one transaction. Clean versions outside the dependency closure remain usable. Rebuilds get new identities; purge removes obsolete text-bearing node records. Local journal/Codex files, grant and action invalidation, backups and the 24-hour purge deadline belong to WP3/4/16. Already sent provider copies and independently stored later replies are outside this memory guarantee.
- Requests capture pins, head, generation and cutoffs in one snapshot. Historical coverage, tail and active source IDs partition the selected prefix. A large tail has **one** bounded range placeholder. Uncollapsed tail items keep their exact source payload. Active tool-result paging preserves IDs and metadata; a modeled call/result pair must stay ordered and paired. Request cost includes fixed system/tools/framing, a 16,384 output reserve, pins, view, tail and active input. The final result is admitted or locally blocked. The provider guard rechecks the generation and pin revision, a trusted stamp over the complete request, and the budget. Spend reservations belong to WP3.

### Properties

Relational properties compare `s` with the ghost pre-state `before`, captured by `tick`. `safety` includes the 26 original WP2 names, the five G1–G4 additions, `pinsSurviveCompaction` and `freshPublicationKept`; `invariants` also includes the finite drain bound.

| --- | --- |

| `redactionMonotone` | Tombstones and the redaction generation never roll back within this archive history. |
| `nodeIdentityStable` | An existing node identity never changes payload or inputs; rebuilds use another version. |

| `restartPreservesHead` | Restart reloads the exact committed partition, versions and coverage. |
| `mergePreservesCoverage` | A merge replaces two siblings by their parent and removes exactly one line without moving coverage. |
| `mergeDueOrder` | The selected pair has greatest due priority measured from its last message, with the oldest tie winner. |
| `prefixBetweenBatches` | Outside merges, removal and masked-line replacement, the old rendered lines remain a prefix. |

The five temporal formulas are **typechecked only**: `eventuallyProjected`, `eventuallySummarized`, `retryEventuallyRuns`, `redactionEventuallySettles`, and `batchEventuallyFinishes`. Their antecedents require permanent quiet, fair recovery, storage and compaction allowance, and eventually successful model calls. `batchEventuallyFinishes` also requires a feasible low-mark partition; its explicit witness is four sources with a built root no larger than the low mark. No hard 512-byte node limit, always-achievable low watermark, unbounded liveness proof or semantic forgetting is claimed.

### Memory bounds and plan clarifications

The simulator instance has ten possible source IDs and aligned widths 1, 2, 4 and 8. Random admission stops at four sources (seven possible built intervals); the ten-source fixture exists for the exact due-order witness. Worker capacity remains eight. View watermarks are reduced to 2,048/1,024 bytes so short histories exercise batching; pin, context, input, tail, active and output-reserve limits keep their production byte values. Random model outputs are 512, 2,048, 8,192 or 20,000 bytes, with arbitrary provenance subsets and an invented marker. This exercises both soft-target failures and the hard cut/copy cap.

The runner takes 1,000 samples of up to 140 transitions with seed `20261013` and two simulator threads. Directed schedules take at most 80 transitions. The 28 corrected schedules share one 512-sample run; the runner requires a completed witness for every case, so a missing sample is a failure. Each broken schedule gets its own evaluator run and counterexample, sharing the parsed/typechecked spec with the other single-sample schedules. A 64-transition drain covers up to seven pending intervals, outstanding/stale jobs, retry deadlines, projection, ordered publication, removal rebuilds, purge and feasible merges. It assumes no new inputs, edits, crashes or failures after `seal`; successful draining outputs are 512 bytes. Quiet recovery is exercised through the actual worker actions. A positive directed trace must end with `label = finished`.

Apalache uses two sources, two workers and the same actions. It checks archive projection, removal, queue durability and restart (`boundedSafety`) for four transitions. Separate broken/corrected `boundedRace` schedules check the six-transition `redactionRace` through the same worker actions. The symbolic schedules omit the general string-command decoder. Quint otherwise sends type metadata for unused declarations and exceeds Apalache’s 20 MB JSON-string limit. The runner uses the pinned Quint compiler in a timed child, retaining only analysis metadata whose IDs occur in the compiled main module. It leaves the compiled module, expressions and types of all live IDs unchanged before calling the normal Apalache backend. No transaction is replaced by a proof-only action. ## Juno Agent actions (WP3)

`agent-actions.qnt` models plan v3 §§6 and 15. `actionsProtocol` supplies the shared transactions; `agent_actions` uses two child actions and `agent_actions_bounded` one. WP15 implements it in `packages/agent/src/{actions,grants,policy}` (see that package's README, "Actions"); the code is tested against the same rules, not verified by this model.

Intents, clicks, consumed authorizations, attempts and receipts are distinct durable records. Executable identity is the complete `Payload` record: connection, account, destination, tool, schema, canonical arguments, precondition and ordered operations. Equality abstracts collision-free JCS hashing. Fresh integer identities stand for globally unique UUIDs, not a shared cross-device allocator; UUID generation itself is outside the model. Edits append immutable revisions. A batch click stores the exact ordered child intents and expands child approvals with a shared batch ID. Authorization checks that expansion against the stored order; each child retains its own consumed authorization and outcome. `batchOrderRace` rejects a reordered expansion. The card preview is assumed to render the stored payload; this spec cannot check pixels or serialization.

`authorizeDispatch`, `transportSend`, `destinationAccept` and `recordReceipt` are separate. A consumed authorization permits one handoff. A crash before handoff still becomes unknown at startup. A negative lookup explains the uncertainty without clearing it. A card runs once: a child whose destination does not deduplicate is authorized at most once (`noRepeatAuthorization`), whatever its first attempt's outcome, because the chat offers no retry (plan v3 O2). A deduplicated child may be authorized again under its key; no v1 card offers that. Deduplication is assumed only for the explicit local destination contract, with stable payload and destination-scoped key for the entire modeled history; it is not asserted for Slack, T3 or generic MCP. Remote traffic already authorized may complete after Pause. Cancellation cannot erase earlier success. Evidence is per attempt: success from an earlier attempt never hides an interrupted retry of the same child (`attemptEvidence`).

Grant scope includes target, operation class, source and revision. Reports and wakes taint a run. No-click grants require a clean owner run and a source in that run; later taint forces a card. The model includes revocation on Pause, removal and schedule deletion. A removal revokes only the grants whose minting run saw the removed content (the ghost `saw`, set by `see`) and carries the rest into the new generation. Resume (or AI assist back on) clears Pause and restores no grant: every live grant was minted since the last Pause (`pauseRevokesGrants`). A click that waited through Pause is authorized once after Resume, if it is still current (`resumedClickWitnessStep`). A persisted occurrence is keyed by schedule, revision and due time; admission and starting it both revalidate the live grant. Quiet hours gate notifications, not authorizations. All billable paths use one reservation ledger, kept per local day as WP9 keeps it; unknown spend retains its reservation on its day. Eight abstract cost units are the initial total cap, and a dollar is four units. Admission keeps compaction within 25% of the cap plus owner raises; chat may use the whole cap. Each call reserves two units, or zero for a free model. A settled reservation counts its true cost, compaction included; an open or unknown one counts its full amount. A day at or over its cap admits nothing. A ghost `charged` records what the provider billed, and `capHard` requires that no reservation records less than that. Random exploration settles with reported costs up to one unit above the reservation, refusals, and losses with any charge up to the reservation; it also raises the limit, starts new days and lowers the cap.

**Auto-review of connection tools (10-16).** A connection tool not on its read list (`proposeConn`, an intent with `conn`) may run with no click when the reviewer and the owner's rule allow it. The reviewer (`reviewWith`) is one decision-model call per intent revision; it answers how explicit the owner's intent was (`explicit`, `implied`, `notAsked`, or `failed` when the call failed or timed out) and whether the call can be undone. The owner sets a rule (`auto`, `always` ask or `never` ask) on the connection (`setRule`) and on the tool (`setToolRule`); the call follows the tool's own unless it is Auto, else the connection's (`ruleOf`). `changeTool` stands for the server changing the tool or adding it: the tool becomes `fresh`, and a fresh tool does not follow the connection's Never ask until the owner sets either rule again. Auto runs only an explicit, reversible review; Always ask never runs without a click; Never ask runs a call from a clean run, or one the owner's words at least implied and the review found reversible. An intent with `hint` (`proposeHinted`, a tool the server marked read-only at first sight) runs with no review only from a clean run, and never under Always ask. Each review records the rule it saw, but authorization reads the rule in force then (`reviewAllows` on the current state). A call the review let run is authorized at most once, as the code's `runReviewed` refuses a second authorization; random exploration found the model missing this guard once read-only marks made such calls common. Each intent records whether its run was clean, and a ghost `cause`: `untrusted` when only text the run read asked for the call, which needs a tainted run. The reviewer sees only the owner's words, so it answers `notAsked` for such a call. `reviewGated` checks every no-click connection authorization against the rule and the review of that exact revision; `untrustedNeverAuthorizes` checks that none came from untrusted text alone, under any rule. Assumption: the reviewer's risk answer is taken as given; the code adds a floor (a tool or operation named like a delete is never reversible), which the model does not check. The code also makes a delete's rule Always ask and never offers a tool that merges, buys or force-pushes; the model does not distinguish tools by kind. `allWritesGated` now names the three authorities a write may run under: a click, a Tier 2 grant, or a passing review of a connection tool.

All 25 named WP3 safety properties in §15 are in `safety` (the plan calls the original list “22” but replaces its quiet-hours entry). Relational checks read the actual ghost pre-state. They cover immutable intents; exact/current/consumed approvals; no hidden retry; no repeat authorization; uncertainty; stable dedupe identity and effects; receipt binding; grant scope and clean runs; destructive-operation refusal; all write paths; local ownership; Pause and off; notifications; schedules and occurrence races; independent children; evidence retention; and the hard cap. WP15 adds `pauseRevokesGrants`. WP16 adds `redactionRevokesExposed` and `redactionKeepsUnrelated`. Auto-review adds `reviewGated` and `untrustedNeverAuthorizes`. `currentOwnerAuthorizes` checks the local authority only. The stronger cross-machine claim is intentionally refuted by WP4.

## Juno Agent recovery (WP4)

`agent-recovery.qnt` uses `recoveryProtocol` for simulator, schedules and the bounded instance. Two local processes contend for one SQLite `owner.lock`; devices A and B have independent local ownership records. The model uses the S5 exclusive SQLite connection named in plan §2.2. Its handle stays strongly referenced until owned I/O stops. Process death closes it. Collection of an unreferenced connection and early shutdown are separate failure pairs.

Inbox admissions are authoritative before acknowledgement. Reset, journal submission, submitted marking, projection and crashes are distinct transitions. Stable operation IDs deduplicate a resubmission after a crash between pi submit and the inbox update. The serial bridge looks up the operation first and skips reset and submit if pi already holds it. A reset before resubmission would end a resumed tool round. A crash before the first submit can leave an extra idle reset, which is harmless. Projection appends the next entry commit and advances the incarnation's cursor atomically. Partials and task-state commits add no entries; their sequence numbers leave gaps and do not move the cursor. A drain means no entry commits remain after the cursor, not `cursor == nextSeq - 1`. The model represents an entry commit by one archive marker, not one message row. Reset transcript records and entry rendering are abstracted. In code, an entry commit can render zero or many messages/chunks and still advance the cursor. An entry's `(incarnation, position)` stands for archive, task and action-origin references. Rotation never reuses an incarnation or its cursor. Existing archive entries survive it; abandoned submissions remain interrupted. All Juno authority commits survive ordinary crashes. FULL here assumes the spike's macOS `fullfsync` and `checkpoint_fullfsync` settings; this is not a disk durability test.

A rolled-back journal is a separately injected older file. Juno reads `durable_metadata.next_seq`, treats the last committed position as `next_seq - 1`, and compares it with the same incarnation's archive cursor before resume. The spike stores an exclusive `next_seq` watermark in its test variable; production must compare the same convention on both sides. A journal at or ahead of projection resumes; a journal behind it rotates and interrupts submitted work. The file must also record the expected incarnation. Each authorization also raises the incarnation's authorized position to the journal's last commit (`authorizedSeq`), and a journal behind that rotates too. The cursor alone detects lost projected commits, not lost trailing pi state; the replay slice below checks why the authorized position is needed. Unsupported schemas stop without replay. `journalAheadStep` and `unsupportedSchemaStep` are explicit positive witnesses.

Cooperative transfer stops admissions, classifies unsettled attempts as unknown, drains projection and local I/O, commits local release, captures the final checkpoint containing that release, publishes release, then installs the successor at the next epoch. The successor reads the released artifact; it cannot read the predecessor’s current local ownership record. `cooperativeTransferStep` exercises that full drain. Restores use a fresh staging database and a new incarnation; approvals are void, pending work is quarantined, and attempts without receipts remain unknown. Review and owner attestation are separate from activation. Activation requires a completed staged install when restoring. Trusted deletion records come from the local keychain, a code-confirmed move or the current strict recovery phrase. Without one, Review discloses that the newest authenticated backup in the folder may restore removed content. `reviewedRestoreStep` witnesses a valid reviewed activation. Forced recovery does not pretend that the attestation shuts down the old machine: the two expected stale-owner counterexamples deliberately leave it alive. Their safe counterparts actually turn it off. Cooperative transfer is the alternative that does not depend on a false attestation.

Backup positions count captured data transactions; export bookkeeping does not recursively create another delta. Backup writes encryption output, publishes an authenticated manifest, then persists the export cursor. A segment is keyed by generation, exact from/to positions and predecessor hash. Integers abstract collision-free hashes; a Boolean abstracts successful authentication; a monotone nonce allocator abstracts fresh random nonces across crashes. Restore stages a gap-free chain and atomically installs only after rechecking the deletion generation. A manifest may arrive before its segment; that is a wait, not permission to skip a gap. Sanitized generation snapshots exclude removed sources and use a fresh generation key. Old keys can be destroyed only after the replacement snapshot is available. Cipher implementation, SQLite changesets, migrations, keychain atomicity, iCloud trash and the five-minute recovery-point service objective need implementation tests. No forensic or cloud erasure claim is made.

All ten WP4 names appear in the model. The nine achievable original properties are in `safety`. The two stated limits, `staleOwnerCannotAuthorize` and `staleFolderCannotRestoreRemoved`, are excluded from unconditional safety. Their expected failures are `partitionedOwnerBugStep` (also `epochReadRaceBugStep`) and `staleFolderBugStep`. Corrected and random runs use `cooperativeSafety`, which includes both. Additional invariants check retained lock handles and a held lock before local store writes, rollback-before-resume, bridge dedupe, export ordering, nonce uniqueness, release inside the checkpoint, stopping after observing a newer owner, uncaptured export bookkeeping, recording deletion before removal, sanitized snapshots and key availability.

### WP8 alignment

These verdicts apply to the code in `packages/agent/src/{runtime,store,inbox,projection,recovery}`. The specs model each departure as the code does it, with a bug schedule for the old rule.

1. **Cursor: safe with the spec change.** `commitsAfter` selects entry commits, and `projectCommit` commits archive rows, memory keys and the cursor together. `projectionPrefix` now compares ordered entry prefixes, using a ghost history that survives file rollback; it no longer equates a sequence number with a row count. `cursorGaps` projects positions 2 and 4, leaves a trailing state commit at 5, crashes, resumes and projects again without duplication. It then completes transfer with cursor 4 and `nextSeq` 6. The old equality drain stalls and violates `drainBound`. State-only commits also take part in random exploration. Drain still assumes the producer has stopped before the final checkpoint.

2. **Trailing-state rollback: unsafe as WP8 shipped it, fixed here.** `agent-recovery-replay.qnt` models pi's tool task as `harness/tool.js` in pi-durable 1.2.0 runs it. The call phase commits an execute intent, a state commit with no transcript entry, before `execute()`. On recovery, a call with an intent and the default `replay: "unsafe"` settles as interrupted; a call without one runs its call phase again. WP8 allocates a fresh authorization on every `authorize`.

   In `trailingState`, the model's tool call is projected (cursor 1) and a copy of the journal is taken. pi commits the intent (2), the tool authorizes and sends, and a receipt is recorded. The copy is then restored over the file. It lost only the intent, so `1 >= cursor 1` and WP8 resumed. pi saw no intent, ran the call again, and the tool obtained authorization 2 and sent a second time. `trailingStateBugStep` violates `authorizedOnce`, and the same trace violates `noRepeatedEffect`. The incarnation check does not help: the copy is of the same incarnation. Reusing authorization 1 could not send again (`replayUnknown`): the hole is the fresh authorization, not `recordAttempt`.

   **Fix.** `authorize` takes the journal position, read by the tool inside `execute()` after pi committed the intent (`Core.position()`). The same transaction raises the incarnation's `authorized_seq` to it, and refuses if the incarnation has been retired. Recovery rotates a journal whose last commit is below `max(cursor, authorized_seq)`, as it does for one behind the cursor. A resumed journal therefore holds the intent of every call that was authorized, and pi settles such a call as interrupted instead of running it again. `trailingStateStep` rotates with one authorization and one effect. `copyAfterIntent` restores a copy taken after the intent and resumes: the check is no stricter than it needs to be. The main recovery model applies the same floor in `openJournal`.

   This is the simplest safe option. Recording every pi commit in `agent.sqlite` would need a write per commit, and it still races: an effect can happen after the intent commit and before the watermark write. Binding authorizations to a stable call identity would need WP15's dispatcher and a key that survives a lost model turn. The authorized position needs one column, one update in a transaction that already exists, and one comparison at open.

   The fix rests on two conditions, each with a bug schedule. A tool with an effect must keep pi's default `replay: "unsafe"`; `unsafeReplayBugStep` declares it safe, and an ordinary crash then authorizes twice. The tool must read the position after the intent commit, that is, inside `execute()`; `earlyPositionBugStep` reads it one commit earlier and authorizes twice. The fix does not restore lost partials or other lost pi state, and it does not detect a copy of a different branch of the same incarnation that is ahead of the authorized position. Such a branch never held the authorized call, so it cannot run it again, but its own model turns may repeat reads and cost.

3. **Publication revision: safe with the spec change.** `publish` validates generation and the exact input digest inside `BEGIN IMMEDIATE`, inserts the node, then reads and advances the head in that transaction. Parallel jobs may start at the same revision and both publish. `parallelLeaves` refutes the old start-revision rejection; `casRace` refutes reusing that revision for the new head. `parallelRedaction` publishes one leaf, removes its source, then tries the other leaf whose raw context included it: the generation/input check discards the stale result. All existing taint, child-input, masked-view and stale-request invariants remain enabled. Job revisions in the model are diagnostic witnesses for the old rule, not a production job requirement. Separate model `extend`/`merge` steps abstract the view work that WP8 batches inside publication.

4. **Bridge reset: safe with the spec change.** `bridgeToolReplay` crashes after submission during a tool round, opens the journal, delivers the same op, marks it submitted and projects twice. The corrected path keeps the tool round, one journal admission and one archive entry. The old fresh-reset path violates `bridgePreservesRun`. `bridgeReplay` still refutes missing request-ID dedupe. This assumes WP8's single writer and serial delivery; it does not authorize concurrent reset calls or resets for a different op during a live run.

The agent checks now contain **113 broken/corrected pairs: 30 memory, 48 actions and 35 recovery** (31 main recovery plus four replay pairs). The `trailingState` trace checked against `noRepeatedEffect` is a second failure witness, not another pair. The replay suite samples 64 traces, requires all four final-state witnesses and checks every replay property in every corrected case. The main recovery suite still samples 1,024 corrected traces; the memory suite samples 512. Existing random runs, finite drains and 19 bounded checks remain. The replay slice is directed simulation only, not an exhaustive or unbounded proof. `packages/agent/test/recovery.test.ts` runs `trailingState` and `unsafeReplay` against the real core and pi.

### WP9 spend alignment

These verdicts apply to `packages/agent/src/spend/` and the settlement calls in `packages/agent/src/providers/guard.ts`. Each departure has a pair. `parallelBudget`, `unknownSpend` and `compactionBudget` still refute their bug modes, and the corrected model keeps `capHard` as restated below.

1. **Settled compaction counts its actual cost: safe.** The compaction sub-budget sums actual costs of settled compaction reservations and full amounts of open or unknown ones, as the `spend.snapshot` query does. `compactionSettle` reserves a compaction, settles it at one unit and is refused a second reservation (`1 + 2 > 2`). The bug mode forgets the settled actual, admits the second reservation and violates `capHard`.
2. **A refusal before output settles as a known zero: safe, if the provider does not bill such refusals.** The guard settles zero only when the request failed locally before it was sent, or failed before any output with a rate limit, a rejected key or another 4xx status from the response's headers (a policy refusal, a bad request, too long, an unknown model, no credits); a 4xx is only that when the SDK's error starts with the status, so an error inside a 200 stream, which may have been billed, keeps its reservation. The engine settles the same way (`settledUsd` in `@juno/ai`), and a call the provider refused for good is not retried, so it settles at the first attempt's bound when it is not a 4xx. A fallback model's call is a second request with its own reservation and settlement, so the model's `refusal` and `lost` outcomes cover it unchanged. `refusedSpend` fills the day, settles one refusal at zero, loses a stream after output, and fits one more reservation in the freed space; the lost stream keeps its full amount. The bug mode settles the lost stream as a refusal; the provider charged it, and `capHard` fails.
3. **Daily reset and the once-per-click raise: safe.** A reservation belongs to the local day it was made on, and its settlement stays on that day. A new day starts at the default cap. `dailyReset` fills day 0, loses one stream, fills day 1, then settles a day-0 reservation: day 0 keeps the unknown amount and gains the actual cost, and day 1 stays full. The bug mode settles onto the current day and puts day 1 over its cap. `allowDollar` inserts one row per click id; `raiseClick` delivers the same click twice and gets one dollar. The bug mode counts both deliveries and violates `oneRaisePerClick`. `capHard` measures against one dollar per distinct click. Lowering the cap (`setCap`) is allowed below existing obligations. Random exploration checks that new reservations still obey the current cap; a ghost records removed allowance for earlier reservations.

4. **The engine's model calls share the cap with a half share: safe.** Topic art, titles, Home and the send checks use the owner's OpenRouter key, so they reserve on the ledger's `engine` path (`packages/engine/src/aiSpend.ts`, answered in `packages/agent/src/start.ts`). The path counts like compaction (settled at actual cost, open and unknown in full) and may use at most half of the day's limit, so chat always keeps the rest. `engineBudget` reserves on the engine path until its share is full, is refused the next one, then fills the day with chat; its bug mode `engineUncapped` drops the share and breaks `capHard`. The engine offers each ask until the agent answers it and the agent applies an ask once by its id; the model does not need that, since each reservation in it is one step. The engine's backlog (art for topics without activity in the last day, Home's checks, the automated channel check) asks as `background` and may use three quarters of that half, counted against everything the engine holds, so titles for new threads, their art and the owner's Redraw keep the last quarter. `engineBackground` fills the backlog's share, is refused the next backlog ask, then still admits a foreground engine call; its bug mode `backgroundUncapped` lets the backlog take the whole half and breaks `capHard`.

**Over-report: the ledger keeps the true cost and stops admitting.** `settle` records the provider's reported cost, even when it is more than the reservation. The reservation prices input at the highest rate and output at the full output reserve, so this needs the provider to count more input tokens than the byte bound allowed; the guard then raises that provider's multiplier to 1.25 for later requests. Decision (WP11 implements it in `packages/agent/src/spend/`): the ledger never records less than was charged, and once the day is at or over its cap every new reservation is refused until the next day or a raise. `capHard` states what then holds: the ledger records at least the charge; no reservation is admitted while the day is at or over its cap (`noAdmissionOverCap`); and the day, and its compaction share, exceed the owner's limit only by allowance removed through cap reductions and costs reported beyond reservations. Only requests already in flight when the day filled can add to that overrun. `overReportStep` keeps the counterexample to the old rule `strictCap` (the cap is never passed): four two-unit reservations fill the day and one settles at three units, nine against eight. `overReportCap` continues that day: a two-unit and a zero-cost reservation are both refused; its bug mode admits them. `capReached` fills the day exactly and refuses a zero-cost reservation; its bug mode is the previous check, which only compared the sum and admitted it. Random exploration reports costs up to one unit above the reservation and includes zero-cost reservations. One limit remains: a stream whose usage is never reported keeps exactly its reservation, so the model assumes the provider billed it at most that. An over-charge on such a stream cannot reach the ledger.

It ran all 114 agent broken/corrected pairs, the random runs and corrected suites of every protocol, and all 19 bounded checks. Every broken variant produced its named violation, `overReportStep` refuted `strictCap`, and every corrected case finished. The twelve-minute budget still holds, with less margin than before. Root `bun run typecheck`, `bun run lint`, `bun run test:agent` (36 tests) and `bun run test:agent-testing` (37 tests) also passed.

`agent-actions.qnt` also contains `agent_resolve`: the corrected/broken pair checks that Undo retains the host receipt, so a repeated dispatch cannot resolve the topic again.

`retryRefused` (decision of 10-18) is the owner's Retry in the topic on a send Slack refused: the outbox posts it as the owner's own send, the agent makes no attempt, and its card takes the receipt. `effectsWithinAttempts` says a send lands at most once per attempt Juno made; the `retryTwice` bug, a destination that takes the retried post twice, breaks it.

`agent-actions.qnt` also contains `agent_host_gate` (decision of 10-18): the host's own check on writes to Slack, under an agent that may dispatch any action, payload and authorization. ## Request pacing

`requestqueue.qnt` models per-method background clocks (a 1,500 ms method like history and a 6,000 ms one like search), the total background ceiling (500 ms, 120/minute), the interactive bucket (20 tokens, 2/second), and unpaced sends. Each tick is 125 ms; four credits make one interactive token. Retry-After doubles a method interval once per pause window up to 60 s; five quiet minutes halve it down to that method's base. `methodSpacingStep` starts one method again at once while the other waits: the other goes after the ceiling, the first only after its own spacing (`methodSpaced`); `methodSpacingBugStep` checks only the ceiling and must break it. Admission follows the lane wait. `invariants` checks pauses, background deadlines, token bounds and interval bounds. The simulation does not model HTTP latency, lane fairness or Slack's actual limits; fake-server tests check the dispatch timestamps. Existing follow/read/catch-up models already permit reordered HTTP responses and make no fixed-spacing assumption.

`discovery.qnt` describes a partial search page with a root and reply, or a reply alone. Applying root metadata first avoids a lookup only in the first case. Search leaves reaction and follow state unchanged. The cold boot's preview search (`searchPreviews`) applies its pages the same way, so the same rules hold for it. A reply a search page stored is partial (no reactions, files or blocks): opening its thread does not count it as stored, so the open fetches the replies page that brings it in full (`openedHasFull`; `partialOpenBugStep` counts the match as stored and must produce a counterexample). `lateIndexedStep` models a reply that Slack indexes after Juno advances its local cursor, with a timestamp three units before it. The five-unit overlap retains the reply; the exact-cursor bug drops it. The model abstracts the counters and lookup success; it does not prove search completeness or replace the merge tests.

## Huddle lifecycle

`huddle.qnt` specifies the `Huddle` service in `@juno/huddle`, for one user, two workspaces, two channels/rooms, three fresh attendee IDs and up to four renderer page generations. Its queue holds at most two room frames and two page reports in flight. Start and Join both call `rooms.join`; successful answers carry a channel, call and attendee identity. A switch waits for the old leave to be sent and its media to be known stopped before issuing the next join. A leave during a request cancels its intent, not its response handler: a successful late answer still needs cleanup.

Two processes with their own lifetimes share the call. The renderer page runs the media (`media`, the checked truth) and can end at any point, before a hand-off too (`pageEnd`; a whole-app `crash` ends it with the engine). The engine never sees the media. It knows main's report of the live page and the highest generation main gave out (`mainReport`, in order, possibly late; every generation at or below it that is not live is gone), the page's whole-state reports (`pageReport`: the session it holds, the last credentials it received, the last session it ended and why), which are delivered in any order and re-sent whenever the page's connection to the engine opens, the engine connection each report and each Start came on, and the order of its own hand-offs. The page's connection can close and reopen at any time (`pageReconnect`); answers and reports still on the old one are lost, and the engine learns of the close later (`seeClosed`). From all that it computes whether an attendee's media is stopped (`engStopped`): no page got the credentials, the page is gone, or the page's latest report sent after the hand-off (on a later connection, or on the same one naming the credentials) does not hold it. Every rule acts on that, never on `media`. A join answered after its Start's connection is known closed is handed to no page. At restart each workspace's stored leaves load asynchronously (`load`), after any number of reports, and no join is sent until all have; the user can switch workspace (`switchWs`) while the page still holds the old one's attendee.

The other actors are the user, Slack HTTP and socket delivery. The model includes quit, a whole-app crash, an engine-only crash (the page keeps its media) and restart; join errors; unawaited leave answers (`ok`, `feature_not_enabled`, or `refused`: a rate limit, the network or the session kept the leave from Slack, so it is pending and unsent again, even after it was cleared); Chime eviction (`AudioJoinedFromAnotherDevice`) and connection loss; late, duplicate and unrelated `sh_room_join`, `sh_room_leave` and `sh_room_update`; reconnect; `Overflowed`; and `huddles/info` request snapshots, failures and stale answers, from an edge that lags the socket (`served` trails `slack` until `catchUp`). `huddle-room.qnt` separately checks the retained room: missing reads preserve it, matching ordered frames update it (including an empty roster), other rooms cannot replace it, and leave or a new attendee clears it. It also checks that the alone rule requires self alone and no remote media presence. It abstracts timestamps to ordered versions and the ten-minute duration to its expiry action; the fake-server tests check the timer and read scheduling. `pending` and each record's `holder` page are durable. Intent, in-flight HTTP, hand-offs and reports in flight, the engine's view of pages and reports, and queued socket frames are volatile. `received`, `sent`, `handed` and `cancelled` are checker history, not proposed database columns.

### States and service events

| Model | Service |
| --- | --- |
| `idle`, `choose`, `requestJoin` | Accept Start/Join from a page, remember its channel, and issue one request after cleanup, once main lists the page. |
| `joining`, `answerJoin`, `handOff`, `cancelStart`, `joinError` | Await Slack and durably record the returned identity (`answerJoin`); then, in a step of its own, hand the credentials to the asking page if the join was not cancelled, main still lists the page and the Start still waits (`handOff`). A Start can be cancelled between the two without the lock (`cancelStart`); then, as when the page is gone, the record names no page and is left at once. |
| `receive`, `pageStop`, `pageReport`, `deliver`, `pageEnd`, `mainReport` | The page decides on credentials when they arrive (start or never), stops sessions the call no longer names or Chime ends, and reports its whole state; the latest report per page wins. |

| `leaving`, `leave`, `quit`, `sendLeave`, `clearLeave`, `leaveAnswer` | Leave stops the page's media and sends `rooms.leave` at once; the stored record is cleared only once the media is known stopped (and its request has gone out: `sendLeave`). Never wait for the HTTP answer; a refusal brings the record back. |
| `crash`, `engineCrash`, `restart`, `load` | Lose process state and replay every workspace's durable records before joining again. A page report that comes before the load still counts after it. A send before a crash can be sent again. |

| `roomFrame`, `disconnect`, `reconnect`, `overflow` | Invalidate the running list. Room frames never end local media. |
| `settle`, `catchUp` | After a join answer, frame, reconnect, overflow or restart, wait until the socket has been quiet for the settle time before reading. Slack's edge catches up meanwhile. |
| `fetchInfo`, `answerInfo`, `infoError` | Fetch a snapshot; discard answers invalidated by a later socket gap or frame, and refetch. |

`phase` is the UI-facing lifecycle. `current` is the engine's active identity; `media` is what the page actually runs.

### Properties and checks

| Requirement | Safety predicate and directed trace |
| --- | --- |
| No ghost | `noGhost`: every received identity has a pending record or a sent leave; any media running on the page, or credentials on their way to it, has its record; drained work is cleaned. `ghostStep` crashes live media; `crashBeforeMediaStep` and `crashAfterLeaveStep` cover the two sides of handoff and a repeated leave on restart. |
| One call | `oneCall`: at most one media session, no join request while media runs or credentials are on their way to the page, and a request starts only after pending cleanup. `oneCallStep` switches channels. |
| Leave during join | `leaveDuringJoin`: a cancelled response never becomes current or gets credentials; after drain its leave was sent. `cancelStep` leaves before the success answer. |
| No stale leave | `noStaleLeave`: room delivery preserves current identity. `staleStep` switches, delivers an old-room leave, then an earlier-attendee leave in the current room twice. The attendee field is checker provenance, not a field claimed to exist on Slack's frames. |
| Matching state | `matchingState`: a valid snapshot matches Slack if Slack has not changed since capture; after quiet recovery, the running lists agree. `overflowStep` loses a queued change; `reconnectStep` discards a response from before a disconnect; `laggingInfoStep` waits for the edge after a frame. |
| Other device | `otherDevice`: after the engine learns of the eviction it has no current call, requested join or queued join intent. `otherDeviceStep` reconnects the socket after eviction without joining again. Only a new user Start/Join clears this condition. |

`quitStep` covers a benign leave error before media stops; `connectionLostStep` a terminal Chime connection loss; `refusedStep` a failed join; `pageGoneStep` a page reload mid-call; `engineCrashStep` an engine crash while the page keeps its media; `refusedLeaveStep` a leave refused after its record was cleared; `pageDuringJoinStep` a page that dies while its join is out; `earlyReportStep` a restart where the page reports before the stored leaves load; `lostStartStep` a Start whose connection closes while Slack has the join, so the answer is lost; `workspaceRestartStep` a workspace switch mid-call and a restart while the page holds the old attendee; `unlistedPageStep` a page that asks and dies before main lists it; `cancelDuringPersistStep` a Start cancelled while its answer is being stored, its connection still open. Each corrected schedule must witness `reachedAgreement`. Fourteen guarded `BugStep` schedules disable one rule apiece and the runner requires their named invariant to fail: durable recording, switch serialization, cancellation, frame isolation, overflow refresh, eviction handling, re-adding a refused leave, waiting for the edge, and six earlier rules. `laggingInfoBugStep` reads a frame's channel at once, as Juno did before 2026-10-22, so the edge's answer from before the frame undoes it and nothing reads again (`matchingState`). `pageDuringJoinBugStep` hands a join answered after its page went to that page (the page-gone event was already consumed); `earlyReportBugStep` loses a report that came before the load; `lostStartBugStep` places a report only by the credentials it names, so the reconnected page's reports never count; `unlistedPageBugStep` takes a page as gone only once main listed it; `cancelDuringPersistBugStep` decides on the Start before the store, so a Start cancelled during it is recorded as holding credentials no page gets. These five never finish cleanup (`drainBound`). `workspaceRestartBugStep` loads only the active workspace after a restart, so its join overlaps the old workspace's media (`oneCall`). The old rules that act during cleanup are switched on by the scenario's first step (`chooseUnder`, the `rule` field). These are deliberate mutation checks; normal transitions use the corrected rules.

`bun run remote specs:check --huddle` selects this model. The normal suite includes it. The runner typechecks it, samples 1,000 random traces of at most 96 steps, and runs 22 corrected schedules plus 14 expected counterexamples. `--traces` reduces random sampling to 100. No Apalache job is added. This is sampled safety with a bounded recovery check, not exhaustive verification. The `seal` action stops new input/failures, asks to leave and assumes subsequent successful delivery. The real cleanup and fetch actions must finish within 32 steps. `eventualCleanup` and `eventualAgreement` state the corresponding fair-recovery temporal properties and are typechecked only, as in the other sync models.

### Limits and plan clarifications

- Cleanup liveness requires the user eventually leave (or quit/crash), the app eventually restart, the page eventually report and main eventually report, and HTTP/media completion eventually make progress. A permanently live call is allowed; the model cannot promise cleanup while the user stays in it forever. A whole-app crash stops local renderer media immediately; the independent Slack list may still contain the attendee until Chime expires it. That server timeout is inferred, unmeasured and outside the checked cleanup bound. A leave send is not proof that Slack removed an attendee; on arch v2 Chime does that.
- Successful response reception and the pending-record transaction are one model step. No local protocol can durably preserve an attendee ID if power fails between receiving the bytes and committing that ID. The guarantee therefore starts at durable receipt. IDs Slack created but Juno never durably learned are outside it. Before media handoff, a failed record write must keep credentials private and attempt cleanup. The plan's literal “every attendee received” is stronger than this implementable guarantee.
- The live frames identify a user and room, not an attendee. The model conservatively refetches on all room frames and never treats a self-leave or absent member as authority to stop current media. Production merges participants only into the room they name (the live check found room id and call id equal).
- Matching Slack immediately on every info answer is impossible under concurrent changes or a lagging endpoint. A snapshot is Slack's list as the edge served it (`servedVersion`); equality is conditional on quiet Slack and a current recovery generation. `settle`'s guard is a timing assumption: the edge catches up within the settle time (3 s in production, chosen from the spike, where a read about three seconds after a leave listed it; not measured exactly). A longer lag can still show a stale list until the next frame. A reconnect/overflow during a request invalidates it. Missing future events require another resync; this model does not claim completeness of Slack delivery or a polling interval.
- One page lives at a time and main gives generations out in rising order, so every generation at or below the highest given out that is not live is gone. A page decides on credentials when they arrive and never starts them later; that is what lets a report sent after a hand-off speak for it. Main's reports arrive in order; page reports in any order, ordered by their own sequence number. Engine connection numbers rise within one engine process; a page has one connection at a time.
- Across a workspace change the stop barrier holds (no join while an attendee of any workspace may still run media), but a leave stored in another workspace waits for that workspace's session to be sent, so `requestJoin`'s `pending.size() == 0` holds per workspace.
- A refusal that arrives after a crash is lost with the crash (`leaveReplies` is volatile). On a workspace without `huddles_arch_v2` a leave Slack refused just before a crash, after its record was cleared, is not sent again; on arch v2 the media stop is the leave that counts.
- Media codecs, permissions, devices, audio quality, screen share, participant merge, profile renewal, request encoding, credentials and unknown HTTP join outcomes are excluded. A failed or malformed read of the stored leaves holds joins back until a read succeeds; the model's `load` is that read's eventual success. Running huddles are a set of channels, so participant details and multiple call generations in that list are abstracted.

### Implementation notes

- Commit the channel, call, attendee and page generation pending-leave record before handing credentials to media. After the commit, in one step, move the Start from waiting to delivered and hand off only if that succeeds; a Start cancelled meanwhile (its cancel takes no lock) gets nothing. Recheck that main lists the page before the hand-off; if not, the record names no page and is left at once. Keep the join response handler alive after cancellation and during orderly quit.
- Keep page state, not events: main's live-page report with the highest generation it gave out, the latest whole-state report of each page (re-sent on every connection to the engine) with the connection it came on, and each hand-off's connection. Compute whether media is stopped from them whenever needed. A generation above the highest given out is pending: no credentials, no refusal. Hand nothing to a Start whose connection closed. Nothing else assumes a stop; no timeout does.
- At boot, load the stored leaves of every workspace on the Mac, and send no join until all are loaded.
- Leave stops the page's media and sends `rooms.leave` at once; the stored record is cleared only once the media is known stopped (and its request has gone out). Never await the answer to `rooms.leave`; `feature_not_enabled` changes nothing. A leave counts as sent once its request goes out; a refusal before Slack took it keeps or restores the record and sends it again. A switch joins only after the old record is cleared. Replay records before allowing a new join after restart; repeat sends are expected.
- On `AudioJoinedFromAnotherDevice`, clear current and queued join intent and wait for a new user command. A socket reconnect must never restart media.
- Subscribe to `SocketEvents` in the session scope. Treat `Overflowed` exactly as a recovery trigger, with the connection unknown after it (it may have dropped a connect); refetch after reconnect and discard an info response invalidated while in flight. Room frames alone cannot identify the current attendee.

## Full reactor lookups

`reactors.qnt` models one message's full lookup: simultaneous callers share a pending request; a socket update or page observed during the request prevents its answer from overwriting the store; the open dialog retries a superseded answer. A truncated page replaces old membership, even at the same count. Socket adds and removes update the stored full list directly. The model checks the response guard and same-count replacement with corrected schedules and expected counterexamples. Random traces check these safety properties too.

The model abstracts one emoji and four people. It assumes an authoritative full response and ordered delivered events, and models missed events only through the same-count swap. It does not model names, transport failures, cancellation, multiple emoji or arbitrary delayed event order. Engine tests cover shared subscriptions, refused calls and the real store. It makes no convergence claim for permanently missing events or incomplete Slack responses.
